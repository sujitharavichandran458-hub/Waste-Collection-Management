# DATABASE NORMALIZATION
## Waste Collection Management System – Scenario 23

### Introduction
Normalization is the process of organizing database tables to reduce data redundancy and improve data integrity.

### 1. First Normal Form (1NF)
A relation is in First Normal Form when:
- Each column contains atomic values.
- There are no repeating groups.
- Each record can be uniquely identified.

Application:
The AREAS, VEHICLES and WASTE_COLLECTIONS tables store individual values in their columns. Therefore, they satisfy 1NF.

### 2. Second Normal Form (2NF)
A relation is in Second Normal Form when:
- It satisfies 1NF.
- Every non-key attribute depends on the whole primary key.
- There is no partial dependency.

Application:
All three tables use a single-column primary key (id). Therefore, partial dependency does not arise, and the tables satisfy 2NF, assuming the stated functional dependencies.

### 3. Third Normal Form (3NF)
A relation is in Third Normal Form when:
- It satisfies 2NF.
- There are no transitive dependencies of non-key attributes on the primary key.

Application:
Area details are stored separately in AREAS.
Vehicle details are stored separately in VEHICLES.
Collection details are stored in WASTE_COLLECTIONS, which references area and vehicle records using their IDs.

This separation avoids storing repeated area and vehicle details in every collection record.

### Conclusion
The EcoTrack database is organized into three related tables: AREAS, VEHICLES and WASTE_COLLECTIONS. Based on the current table design and assumed functional dependencies, the tables satisfy 1NF, 2NF and 3NF.
